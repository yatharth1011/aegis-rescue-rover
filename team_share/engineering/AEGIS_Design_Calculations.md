## 2. Drive torque and traction

Per-wheel output torque, straight line:

$$\tau_{wheel} = \frac{r}{4}\left(C_{rr}\,m g\cos\theta + m a + m g\sin\theta\right)$$

Two corrections to the formula in the hand-off note:

- It used the traction coefficient μ as a resistive force. μ is the grip limit, not a drag. The drag term is rolling resistance $C_{rr}$. μ is used below only to check for wheel slip.
- It divided by a gearbox efficiency η. The listed motor torques are already measured at the gearbox output and the wheel is mounted directly on the shaft, so no extra η applies.

Skid-steer pivot turn: each wheel scrubs sideways at $\mu_{lat}\,mg/4$ with lever arm L/2. The four drive forces act at B/2:

$$F_{wheel} = \frac{\mu_{lat}\, m g\, L}{4B}, \qquad \tau_{wheel} = F_{wheel}\, r$$

- Traction available per wheel: μ·mg/4 = 1.91 N. The worst drive force is 1.19 N per wheel, so the wheels grip and do not slip (PASS).
- The steepest climbable slope is set by traction, not torque: atan(μ) = 39°.
- Speed at rated load: 0.37 m/s. Pivot turning is the hardest drive case, at 1.4× the torque of the ramp case.

### 3.2 Rail B, 5.0 V servo

MG946R stall is listed at 1.2 A [L]. The rail is sized for 2.5 A [A], because MG99x-class servos commonly measure 2–2.5 A at stall. Measure the real value on a bench supply before trusting either number. The servo is run at 5.0 V, not 6 V, so its stall torque (10.9 kg·cm) keeps the printed gear above SF 2.5 (section 4).

### 3.3 Battery: worst case

Everything stalled at once, battery at 10.5 V loaded:

| Load | Battery-side A |
|---|---:|
| Motors 4 x stall | 4.00 |
| Servo stall (via buck) | 1.40 |
| Logic peak (via buck) | 0.44 |
| **Total** | **5.84** |

- C-rate: 5.84 A / 2.2 Ah = **2.66 C**, against the pack's 10 C rating (PASS).
- A 2C pack (the 999-rupee 2200 mAh INR option) has a 4.4 A limit and would **fail** this case. That is why the BOM uses the 10C ISR pack.
- The pack's BMS continuous rating is not on the listing. It must be ≥ 6 A. Check it on arrival.
- TB6612FNG channel: motor stall is 1.05 A at 12.6 V, against 1.2 A continuous per channel (PASS). This works only with **one motor per channel**, which is why the BOM has two drivers.

The hand-off note's figure was 4(0.45) + 1.2 + 0.25 + 0.1 = 3.35 A ("1.52 C, far below 20 C"). It was wrong in four ways:

1. It assumed 0.45 A motor stall. The listing says 1 A.
2. It assumed 1.2 A servo stall, the optimistic listing figure.
3. It added 5 V and 6 V rail currents directly to battery current, without converting through the buck converters.
4. It assumed the pack was rated 20 C. The pack it named is 2C.

## 4. Gripper strength (hand calculation, to be confirmed in Fusion Simulation)

Mechanism: the servo spline drives the +Y jaw directly. The −Y jaw pivots on an M3 steel pin. The two jaw hubs mesh as a 1:1 spur pair (module 2, 15 teeth, pitch radius 15 mm, face 10 mm), so each jaw sees the full servo torque.

Material: PLA+ printed flat (XY), allowable yield σ_y = 40 MPa [A]. Required SF ≥ 2.5.

Servo stall torque at 5.0 V: 10.92 kg·cm = 1.071 N·m (interpolated from listed 10.5 kg·cm @ 4.8 V and 13 kg·cm @ 6 V).

| Check | Load | Stress | SF | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Jaw arm bending at hub, spec pinch | 10.0 N tip | 4.1 MPa | 9.8 | PASS |
| Jaw arm bending at hub, servo stall | 21.4 N tip | 8.8 MPa | 4.6 | PASS |
| Gear tooth (Lewis), servo stall | 71.4 N tangential | 12.3 MPa | 3.25 | PASS |
| Pivot bore bearing on PLA | 74.5 N | 2.5 MPa | 16.1 | PASS |

- Arm bending: σ = M/Z, with M = F·(L − r_hub) and Z = h·b²/6 (b = 8 mm in the bending direction, h = 8 mm).
- The **gear teeth are the critical feature**, not the jaw arm. A first layout used a 12-tooth, 12 mm-radius gear with the servo at 6 V. It gave σ = 18.4 MPa, SF = 2.18, a **fail**. The fix was to grow the pitch radius to 15 mm (15 teeth) and run the servo at 5.0 V.
- 15 teeth at 20° pressure angle is just under the 17-tooth undercut limit. Generate the teeth with Fusion's SpurGear add-in at 25° pressure angle, or with +0.1 profile shift.
- The torque needed for the 10 N spec pinch is 0.50 N·m, 47 % of stall. **Firmware must stop commanding the servo at the contact angle.** Commanding past contact stalls the servo continuously and heats it.

## 5. Fusion Simulation setup for the jaw (FEA)

Run this after `Build_AEGIS_Rover.py` and after cutting the real gear teeth.

1. **Simulation workspace → New Study → Static Stress.** Simplify: keep only the `Gripper_Jaw_PLA` body; remove or suppress everything else.
2. **Material:** Study Materials → PLA. If the library has no PLA, create a custom material: E = 3.5 GPa, ν = 0.36, ρ = 1.24 g/cm³, **yield = 40 MPa**, ultimate = 45 MPa.
3. **Constraints:**
   - Pin constraint on the 3.2 mm pivot bore (radial + axial fixed, tangential free).
   - Fixed constraint on the flank of one gear tooth at the pitch line. This reacts the drive torque the way the meshing tooth does.
4. **Loads:**
   - Case A: 10 N on the fingertip face, normal to it, pinching inward.
   - Case B: 21.4 N, the servo stall case.
5. **Mesh:** global 5 % of model size. Local mesh control 0.5 mm on the hub-to-arm junction and on the loaded tooth root. Enable adaptive refinement (3 passes).
6. **Pass criteria:**
   - Minimum safety factor (von Mises vs yield) ≥ 2.5 in both cases.
   - Peak stress in case B should be within about 30 % of the hand calculation: arm 8.8 MPa, tooth root 12.3 MPa.
   - If FEA is much higher at the hub-arm corner, add a 2 mm fillet there.
7. **Record for the report:** the safety-factor plot, the von Mises plot, the displacement at the fingertip, and the mesh convergence plot.
